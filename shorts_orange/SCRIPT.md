# History of the orange: draft script (Relicly animated Short)

Reference idea: "How did the orange get its name?" (Remember This, youtube.com/shorts/iF8LEcaaFEA). Our version keeps the idea, not the video.

Voice: vidIQ Liam, [tags] for delivery, played at 1.2x like the Niihau Short. Target about 60-64 s.

## Lines
00 [curious] Before oranges, English had no word for orange.
01 [amused] None. The colour was just... "yellow-red."
02 [deadpan] That's why a robin has a red breast. A fox is a red fox. And ginger hair is "red."
03 The word was born in southern India, as "naranga."
04 Traders carried the fruit west. In Persia: "narang." In Arabia: "naranj."
05 Into Spain. Into Italy. And into France... as "une narange."
06 [playful] Then the French said it too fast. "Une narange"... "une orange." [pause] The N just fell off.
07 [curious] Some say French "or", gold, pulled it the rest of the way.
08 The fruit reached England first. The colour took longer.
09 In 1502, a royal shopping list asked for cloth of "orenge colour."
10 [deadpan] So the fruit isn't named after the colour. The colour is named after the fruit.
11 [warm] And in Greece, an orange is still a "portokali"... after the Portuguese ships that brought the sweet ones.
12 [amused] All that... for a word that lost its N.

## Picture idea (2D characters in a 3D world, one travelling camera)
- Hook: a Tudor painter stares at a fruit bowl, holding a pot labelled YELLOW-RED.
- Robin, fox and a ginger kid, each with a red label that wobbles.
- The journey: one merchant carries the orange and a hanging word-sign west. The camera travels with him, and the sign changes at every stop: Indian orchard, Persian caravan, Arab market, Spanish courtyard, Italian piazza, French market.
- The gag: the French seller says it fast and the N drops off the sign and rolls away.
- A gold coin glints on "or".
- English court, 1502: a scribe writes "orenge colour" on a list, and the painter finally gets a new paint pot.
- Greece: a Portuguese ship unloads sweet oranges, and the dock sign reads PORTOKALI.
- End: the lost N rolling along the dock; it lands at the painter's feet.

## Facts to keep straight
- Path: Sanskrit naranga (likely Dravidian origin) > Persian narang > Arabic naranj > Spanish naranja, Italian narancia/arancia > Old French orenge > English orange.
- The lost n: "une narange" heard as "une orange" (rebracketing); Italian lost it too (arancia).
- The "or" (gold) influence on the French form is a common theory, hence "some say".
- English used "geoluhread" (yellow-red) in Old English. Robin redbreast, red fox and red hair keep the older usage.
- Colour name: commonly cited first record about 1502 (cloth bought for Margaret Tudor). The fruit word is earlier (14th century).
- Greek portokali, also Romanian portocală and Arabic burtuqal, after the Portuguese, who brought sweet oranges in the 1500s.

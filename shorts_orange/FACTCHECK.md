# Fact check: client's locked script (orange / santra)

| # | Line | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fruit left India, came back with a new name | Mostly OK | The *word* started in India (Dravidian > Sanskrit). The fruit itself comes from a region of southern China, north-east India and Myanmar. Safer: "This fruit's name left India". |
| 2 | Sanskrit "naranga" | OK | Wikipedia "Orange (word)"; Etymonline. |
| 3 | Persian "narang", colour named after it | OK | Persian narang; the colour words narenji (Persian) and narangi (Hindi) come from the fruit. |
| 4 | Arabic had no g, so naranj; Portuguese laranja | OK | Encyclopaedia Iranica on Persian loans in Arabic (g > j/q); Wiktionary. |
| 5 | "a narange" > "an orange" | OK, place it right | The n was lost in Italian and French (una narancia > un'arancia). Spanish naranja kept it. |
| 5b | French loved it because "or" means gold | NOT SUPPORTED | Popular claim. Sources only say the French "o" may come from the town of Orange. Cut it. |
| 6 | Portuguese found a sweeter one in China | Roughly OK | Portuguese ships brought the sweet orange from Asia in the 1500s; whether from China or India is disputed. Safer: "from the East". |
| 6b | Peel slips right off | WRONG | The Portuguese sweet orange has tight skin. Loose skin is the mandarin, which is what Indian santra actually is (Nagpur santra = Citrus reticulata). Cut it. |
| 7 | Planted in Sintra, Europe went crazy | Partly | Sintra was known for its orange groves, but there's no source that the sweet orange was first planted there. Safer: "a town famous for its oranges: Sintra". |
| 8 | Dutch and Germans: China's apple | OK | sinaasappel / Apfelsine, via French "pomme de Sine". |
| 9 | 1500s Portuguese to Goa as "orange of Sintra" | Partly | The Portuguese did reach Goa in the 1500s. But the exact phrase "laranja de Sintra" has no source. |
| 10 | Santra named after Sintra | Likely, not proven | Wiktionary (Hindi, Gujarati) and Dawn back it. Wiktionary (Urdu) hedges and also gives Persian "sangtara". Say "most likely". |

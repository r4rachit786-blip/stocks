# SCRIPT: Orange / santra (v11, locked) and the v2 take list

## Voice script (v11, as spoken; delivery tags not spoken)
This fruit left India... travelled the whole world... and got a new name everywhere it went.
In Sanskrit, it was "naranga."
Persian traders took it home as "narang"... and named the color after it too.
Arabic had no "g" sound. So "narang" became "naranj." Then in Portugal: "laranja."
In Europe, "a narange" slowly turned into "an orange"... the N just fell off.
Then the Portuguese sailed to China... and found a sweeter one, sweet enough to eat like candy.
They grew it in a little town called Sintra... and Europe went crazy for it.
The Dutch still call it "China's apple."
In the 1500s, Portuguese ships brought it to India... as the orange from Sintra.
Now say "Sintra" ten times fast...
Santra! That's still the Hindi word for orange today.
So... what do you call it?

Voice: the 12 locked Liam lines (o00-o11), trimmed, pauses tightened, atempo 1.2 -> voice.wav 47.02 s (1411 frames at 30 fps). Word timings: whisper on voice.wav.

## History notes kept honest
- The word goes Sanskrit naranga -> Persian narang (the colour narenji is named after it) -> Arabic naranj (no g sound) -> Portuguese laranja.
- The n was lost in Italian/French (una narancia > un'arancia; a narange > an orange); English took "orange".
- The Portuguese brought the sweet orange from the East in the 1500s; Sintra was famous for its orange groves (not shown as "first planted there").
- Dutch sinaasappel = "China's apple" (via French pomme de Sine). Goa was taken by the Portuguese in 1510 (the quay arch date).
- Santra most likely comes from Sintra (Wiktionary Hindi/Gujarati); no real names on screen.

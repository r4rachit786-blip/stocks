A Pearl Harbor pilot was told to crash on an empty island.
A hundred and thirty-six people were standing on it.
The plan: land on tiny Niihau... and wait for a submarine.
So he lands.
In a fence.
A local man pulls him out, and politely borrows his pistol and his papers.
The pilot wants them back. No luck.
Nobody here knows there's a war... so they throw him a party!
That night, a battery radio delivers the news.
The party ends quickly.
Now he's a prisoner. And no boat can come: the military has banned them all.
Five days later, he finds a helper... with a shotgun.
They burn a house looking for the papers. They burn the plane.
The village hides in caves. One rancher does not.
He jumps the pilot. The pilot shoots him three times.
As he told it later... that's when he got mad.
He throws the pilot into a stone wall.
His wife ends the argument with a rock.
You see, Niihau was never empty. It was private: one family, no phones, no power, one boat a week.
The rancher got a medal. The submarine never came.
And the plane? Still in a Hawaii museum... a souvenir from the island that was supposed to be empty.

# What if the nuclear bomb was never built? (Short, 45-60 sec, fast paced)

_Handed over from the ShortStack session. Chosen angle: "The bomb came later"._

## Hard facts (safe to state on screen)
- Fission discovered Dec 1938, Berlin (Hahn and Strassmann)
- Einstein-Szilard letter to Roosevelt, Aug 2, 1939
- First reactor (Chicago Pile-1) Dec 2, 1942, before any bomb existed
- Trinity test July 16, 1945; Hiroshima Aug 6, Nagasaki Aug 9, 1945
- Deaths by end of 1945: estimates about 110,000 to 210,000 for both cities combined. Use a range, never one number. (Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, Wellerstein)
- About 500,000 Purple Hearts made for the invasion of Japan (Truman Library Institute, D. M. Giangreco)
- Invasion casualty estimates widely disputed: tens of thousands (early staff estimates) to millions (later estimates)
- Soviet first bomb Aug 29, 1949
- Warheads today: about 12,187 in 9 countries at the start of 2026; about 3,912 deployed (Federation of American Scientists). Cold War peak about 70,000 (mid-1980s)
- Nuclear power: 2,850 TWh in 2025, roughly 9 to 10% of world electricity (IEA, Electricity 2026)
- R-7 rocket that launched Sputnik (Oct 4, 1957) was designed to carry a nuclear warhead
- Early computers like ENIAC ran bomb calculations; Monte Carlo method came out of Los Alamos ("sped up", not "created")

**Avoid:** "the internet exists because of nukes" (myth), any single death toll, any "would have happened" stated as fact.

## Imagined timeline
Educated imagination, every leap anchored to a real fact; narration says "in this timeline" / "imagine".
- **1942:** Manhattan Project never approved. Anchor: about $2 billion, over 100,000 people. Imagine: money goes to radar, jets, penicillin; Allied jets in combat a year earlier.
- **Aug 1945:** no flash over Hiroshima. Anchor: Operation Downfall planned Nov 1945, ~500,000 Purple Hearts. Imagine: Nov 1, 1945 the largest amphibious landing in history hits Kyushu; war grinds into 1946; Soviets (already in Manchuria) land on Hokkaido; Japan split into North and South.
- **1950s:** Cold War turns hot. Imagine: no fear of mutual destruction, World War III in Europe around 1950, tanks and bombers.
- **Late 1950s:** no ICBM-driven space race; no Sputnik, no Moon landing in 1969.
- **1960s-70s:** reactors arrive later and smaller; coal and oil fill the gap; climate change faster.
- **2020s twist:** the bomb is finally built in a world that never saw Hiroshima. No taboo, no warning. Loop line: "So maybe the scariest version of history... is the one where the bomb comes later."

**Angle ranking:** 1) "The bomb came later" (chosen), 2) "Two Japans", 3) "World War III in 1950".

## Debate design (agree/disagree)
Take a clear side on genuinely debated questions, never fake a fact.
- Debates: Did the bomb save lives by preventing an invasion? Did nukes keep the peace after 1945? Would someone else have built it? Would the world be better without it (twist: later could be worse)?
- Lines: mid-video "Hot take: the bomb probably prevented World War III"; end "Better world or worse? Comment BETTER or WORSE"; pinned "Historians are split on this. Was Japan about to surrender anyway? Make your case."
- Mention the other side once ("some historians say Japan was already beaten").
